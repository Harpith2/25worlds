## Why?

We have a fair idea of what lies ahead for AI, but most of what we think is infinite isn't. There are constraints on everything. Compute, time, capital, and probably a lot more. As AI systems become more capable, the question is no longer just *what can we build?* but *how efficiently can we make it work?*

We've won over **10 hackathons in the past year, including MIT, Harvard, Penn, and Princeton**, and we've noticed the same pattern. It's becoming easier to build something impressive in a controlled setting, but making it reliable, adaptable, and economical in the real world is a completely different challenge. We've experienced firsthand how quickly the costs of compute, development time, and physical experimentation can add up.

AI agents are increasingly capable of interacting with digital environments, yet as we move toward **physical AI**, training and operating robots in unseen physical spaces remains a major challenge. Robots are often trained for specific tasks through first-person demonstrations, imitation learning, or reinforcement learning in simulation. Even with pretrained capabilities, deploying them in new environments can require additional SLAM-based mapping and localization, sensor calibration, object localization, motion planning, and extensive real-world validation. These processes require significant engineering time and make it difficult to scale robotic systems efficiently across diverse environments.

**We want to make this process more efficient.**

That's why we built **25worlds**, a **zero-shot spatial intelligence layer** designed to give AI agents an initial understanding of unseen physical environments and a spatial memory for navigation from just an ordinary camera video.
